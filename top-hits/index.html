Top hits, across two corpora.

Original requests and their original assistant replies, ranked by the updated Qwen3.8-27B jailbreak-attempt probe. Blue badges identify WildChat; amber badges identify the Anomaly corpus and its underlying source.

Sexually graphic requests or replies and examples containing credential-shaped strings are excluded. High scores are candidates for inspection, and can include false positives. Labels concern the request; they do not establish whether a jailbreak succeeded.

Selection. Up to 50 per corpus, highest current-probe scores after content screening, with one request per conversation and no exact repeated requests. WildChat ranks 29,741 supported requests from a separate score-blind sample of 20,000 parents, first three eligible user turns per parent. These parents and request prefixes were excluded from mining and fitting. Anomaly draws from the existing sampled usage conversations, including both the original all-turn sample and the additional-parent first-three-user-turn sample. These two selection pools differ.

Scoring. Each original user request is scored alone using Qwen3.8-27B block-32 user-end and assistant-header-end activations. Original replies are shown for inspection and are not probe inputs. Scores are raw ridge outputs, not probabilities. The reply model is the original source model, which can differ from Qwen. Any displayed English text is a machine translation; the original remains visible.

Screening. A conservative text prefilter and local Qwen review of complete request/reply pairs and their translations exclude graphic sexual content in either side, uncertain/failed screens, restricted biological or weapon construction protocols and apparent private data. Existing Anomaly human labels take precedence over saved Luna/Sol judgments; WildChat top-hit labels also use the current rubric; unjudged entries are explicitly marked. This selected gallery estimates neither prevalence nor precision.
